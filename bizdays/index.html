Business Days Calculator: Count Working Days the Accurate Way

Ask a project manager how long a task takes and you will usually hear an answer in business days: "That will ship in ten business days." Ask a calendar the same question and it answers in a completely different language — fourteen calendar days, two of which are weekends when nobody is at their desk. The gap between those two numbers is where deadlines slip, contracts get misread, and delivery promises quietly break. A business days calculator exists to close that gap. BizDays counts only the days when work actually happens: Monday to Friday, minus the public holidays and company shutdown days that you specify.

The idea sounds simple, and the arithmetic behind it is simple too — but doing it by hand over a long range is surprisingly error-prone. Counting the working days in a quarter means scanning three calendar months, ruling out twelve or thirteen weekends, and then remembering which holidays fall on weekdays and which ones land on a Saturday and therefore change nothing. One missed bank holiday and every downstream date in your plan shifts by a day. That is why teams in operations, human resources, finance, logistics and legal work lean on a dedicated working-day calculator instead of a spreadsheet guess.

What exactly is a business day?

A business day — also called a working day or workday — is any day on which normal business operations take place. In most countries that means Monday through Friday, excluding public holidays. Saturdays and Sundays form the standard weekend across North America, Europe, India, Australia and much of the rest of the world, and BizDays follows that convention. Some regions historically used a Friday–Saturday weekend, and some industries, such as retail, healthcare and hospitality, staff Saturdays as a matter of routine; for those cases you can treat a listed "holiday" in reverse by simply planning around the standard count, but for the vast majority of office, banking and government work the Monday-to-Friday definition is the one written into contracts and service-level agreements.

Public holidays are the second half of the definition, and they are the half people most often forget. A range that looks like twenty weekdays on the calendar may contain one or two national holidays that remove real working capacity. Worse, a holiday that falls on a weekend is usually not lost — many employers observe it on the nearest Friday or Monday — which is exactly the sort of nuance a hand count mangles. BizDays lets you paste your own holiday list, including any observed dates your company recognises, so the count reflects the calendar your team actually works to rather than a generic one.

Two modes: count between dates, or add workdays to a date

Most working-day questions come in two shapes. The first is a counting question: "How many business days are there between the 3rd of March and the 28th of April?" You might ask it to measure how long a vendor took to deliver, to compute the notice period in a contract, to work out how many days of leave an employee is really taking, or to check whether a support ticket breached its response-time promise. BizDays answers it with the Count Between Dates mode: choose a start and an end date, decide whether those two endpoint days themselves should be included, and read off the business days alongside calendar days, weekend days and holiday days so you can see exactly how the total is built.

The second shape is a projection question: "If we start on Monday and the task needs fifteen working days, when do we finish?" That is the Add / Subtract Workdays mode. Enter a start date and a number of workdays — positive to move forward, negative to count backwards from a deadline — and BizDays walks the calendar day by day, stepping over weekends and your holidays, and tells you the exact date you land on, its day of the week, and how many calendar days the journey actually spans. Backwards mode is a quiet favourite with planners: set the launch date, subtract the workdays a task requires, and you get the latest honest start date instead of an optimistic one.

Why the per-month breakdown matters

A single total is enough for a quick answer, but longer ranges hide useful structure. A three-month project with sixty-three business days does not spread that capacity evenly: one month may carry a cluster of holidays while the next is completely clear. The per-month breakdown in BizDays itemises every month in your range — calendar days, business days, weekend days and holidays side by side, ledger style — so you can spot the thin months before they surprise you. Payroll teams use this view to sanity-check pro-rated salaries and timesheets. Finance teams use it to align billing cycles and revenue recognition with the real number of working days in each period. Anyone planning a phased rollout can see at a glance which month absorbs a holiday-heavy stretch and which month offers a clean run of uninterrupted weeks.

Custom holiday lists and 2026 presets

No two organisations observe exactly the same calendar. A multinational team may follow head-office holidays, a factory may shut down between Christmas and New Year, and a school or government office may follow a regional list with local festivals and state days added. That is why the holiday list in BizDays is fully yours: type or paste one date per line in YYYY-MM-DD format, optionally with a label after the date, and those days are excluded from both calculation modes instantly. If a holiday in your list falls on a Saturday or Sunday, BizDays does not double-count it — it is already a non-working day, and the ledger shows it honestly in the month rows without inflating the weekday-holiday total.

To save typing, one-tap presets load the main 2026 national holidays for the United States federal calendar, for England, and for India. Treat presets as a starting point rather than gospel: add your company's own shutdown days, regional holidays and observed dates on top, and remove anything your organisation does not observe. Because everything runs locally in your browser, you can experiment freely — tweak the list, watch the totals update, and settle on the calendar that matches your real working year.

Everyday situations where a working-day count keeps you honest

Consider a few ordinary scenarios. A freelancer quoting "delivery in 15 business days" for a project starting the week before a national holiday owes the client a finish date, not a guess — adding fifteen workdays with the holiday excluded produces the date that belongs in the proposal. An HR manager approving twelve days of leave that spans two weekends and a public holiday needs to know the employee is actually using nine working days of allowance, not twelve. A logistics coordinator promised "dispatch within 3 business days" needs to know that an order placed on a Friday afternoon ships by Wednesday, not Monday. A lawyer checking a filing deadline expressed in business days cannot afford an off-by-one error caused by a bank holiday nobody noticed. In each case the cost of a wrong count is real — money, trust or compliance — while the cost of checking takes about ten seconds.

There is also a subtle trap worth knowing about: inclusive versus exclusive counting. "Ten business days from Monday" can mean the tenth working day counting Monday itself, or the day reached by moving ten working days forward. Contracts and HR policies differ on the convention, which is why BizDays gives you explicit include-start and include-end toggles in count mode and a clear rule in add mode — the starting day is never counted as a step; each step moves you to the next actual business day. Whichever convention your situation needs, the setting is visible on screen rather than buried in a formula.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as a business day in BizDays?▾

A business day is any Monday to Friday that is not in your holiday list. Saturdays and Sundays are always excluded, and any date you add to the custom holiday panel is excluded too, in both the counting mode and the add/subtract mode.

What happens if a holiday falls on a weekend?▾

Nothing is double-counted. A holiday that lands on a Saturday or Sunday is already a non-working day, so the business-day total stays correct. The per-month table still lists it in the Holidays column, but the "Holidays (Mon–Fri)" figure only counts holidays that actually remove a working day. If your employer observes that holiday on a Friday or Monday instead, add the observed date to your list.

Should I include the start and end dates in the count?▾

It depends on the convention you are following. Counting both endpoints (the default) answers "how many working days does this period contain?", which suits leave balances and timesheets. Untick one or both endpoints when a contract or policy says "within N business days after this date", so the trigger day itself is not counted. The toggles update the totals and the monthly breakdown instantly.

How does the Add / Subtract Workdays mode work?▾

Pick a start date and enter how many workdays to move — a positive number travels forward, a negative number travels backward. BizDays steps one calendar day at a time, skipping weekends and your holidays, until it has landed on that many business days. The start date itself is never counted as a step, and entering zero simply returns the start date.

Can I use my own company or country holiday list?▾

Yes — that is what the Custom Holiday List panel is for. Paste one date per line in YYYY-MM-DD format (a label after the date is fine), use the date picker to add days one at a time, or start from the built-in 2026 presets for US federal, England or India holidays and edit from there. Duplicates and invalid lines are ignored automatically.

Is BizDays free, and is my data private?▾

Yes on both counts. BizDays is completely free with unlimited usage — no signup, no paywall and no watermark. Every calculation happens locally in your browser, so the dates and holiday lists you enter never leave your device and are never stored or tracked.
